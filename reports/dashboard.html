Volatility-Adjusted Dip-Buy / Pop-Short

Strategy analysis & verdict — automated from the Excel workbook, validated on IBKR data. 2026-05-22

Verdict

No demonstrated trading edge. The headline profit is largely a daily-bar fill illusion, not an executable return.

The Excel math is correct — our Python engine reproduces it to the penny. But it decides fills and same-day take-profits on daily bars, which silently assumes an intraday price order that often never happened. Re-checking the exact same trades minute-by-minute erases ~78% of the profit. Every robustness test (out-of-sample, walk-forward, Monte-Carlo) rejects it.

What actually happened, minute by minute

The order of events makes the "win" impossible

1
09:30 — High 193.61. Price spikes above the TP (191.20) at the open. But you have no position yet — your buy sits down at 187.75.
2
11:25 — price falls to your entry. The long fills at 187.75 — almost two hours after the high the Excel row used as its "win".
3
After the fill, the high is only 190.53. The TP (191.20) is never reached again. Price keeps sliding.
4
16:00 — Close 186.94. The real outcome is a loss of ≈ −$0.81/share, not the +$3.45 win in cell AA8. The Excel was off by $4.26/share on this single trade.

Takeaway: a daily bar only knows the day's high and low — not which came first. The Excel's "same-day wins" repeatedly bank a high that occurred before the entry could fill. That is the single biggest reason +26.8% becomes +4.4%.

3 · What the strategy is (in plain terms)

Strategy 1 — Buy (dip-buy)

Bet that a one-σ dip below yesterday's close bounces. Place a buy limit at prevClose − 1σ; take profit at prevClose + 0.5σ; stop 3σ below entry. If profit isn't hit that day, hold and watch the next 20 trading days for TP or stop, whichever comes first.

Candidates: Finviz "strong wedge-up" charts.

Strategy 2 — Short (pop-short)

Mirror image: short a one-σ pop above yesterday's close; cover at entry − 0.75σ; stop 3σ above. Two gates the sheet adds (and we hadn't ported): only short when VIX > 17 and the analyst rating is Sell / Strong Sell.

σ = standard deviation of the daily High−Low range. Size = $100k/trade.

Tuning a strategy to past data and seeing it reverse out-of-sample is the signature of overfitting — no real edge to lock in.

OCO fade — win-rate UP, profit DOWN = fading a trend

0.2σ (tight)13%−$755k

As the stop widens you win more often but lose more money: many small wins, but the occasional trend runs the wide stop for a loss that swamps them. That inversion is the fingerprint of betting against momentum on trending stocks.

5 · What we learned chasing an edge anyway

Direction

Predicting up/down days from price: out-of-sample AUC ≈ 0.50 (a coin flip). The dumb "market always rises" rule beats the model. Dead.

Volatility

Predicting how much it moves: real skill on the index (SPY R² 0.50→0.61 out-of-sample). On single names it's just "vol clusters" — free from a one-line average.

…but tradeable?

We only beat stale 30-day VIX (+11.7%). The real bar is the market-makers' 1-day implied (VIX1D / 0-DTE), net of their spread. In SPX that's Citadel et al. — the wrong arena. Test pending.

The principle that matters: beating a naive baseline is worthless — everyone beats it. An edge means beating the people pricing the trade (the market makers), net of costs. The vol-forecasting machinery is real; it should be aimed where market makers are weak (illiquid options, crypto vol, prediction markets), not at the most efficient market on earth.

6 · Honest caveats on this verdict

Wrong universe

We rejected it on liquid mega-cap tech (NVDA, TSLA, AVGO…). The sheet was designed for Finviz-screened small-cap wedge setups (TIGR, BTAI, SNAP…). The fill-illusion is mechanical and universe-independent, so the verdict holds — but that screened universe was never run end-to-end.

Ungated short

Our short backtest was purely mechanical. The sheet's short has real filters (VIX>17 + Sell rating) we hadn't implemented — so the short was tested in a form stricter than designed.

7 · Bottom line & next steps

  • The strategy: no edge as built/tested; the Excel profit is mostly a daily-fill illusion.
  • The real deliverable already exists: Python automation replacing the manual 2-sec-per-symbol paste loop — watchlist backtesting, next-morning signals, IBKR data, and an honest OOS/walk-forward/Monte-Carlo validator.
  • Two open loops: (a) the VIX1D / 0-DTE straddle P&L test — the true "is the vol skill money?" finish line; (b) the screened small-cap wedge universe — the one environment the strategy was designed for that we never ran.
  • The productive direction: point the vol-forecast machinery where the competition is beatable, not at SPX.
