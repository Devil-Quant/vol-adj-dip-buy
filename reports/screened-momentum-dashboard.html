Screened TP/SL Momentum Backtest — Independent Review

A σ-scaled take-profit/stop strategy on a 33-name Finviz momentum screen (Feb 9 – May 18 2026, $100k/trade), re-tested against realistic fills and a buy-and-hold benchmark. 2026-05-22

Verdict

In an optimistic-fill model it nearly matches buy-and-hold. With realistic fills it captures only ~15% of just holding the same names — before costs.

The original summary reports strong gross numbers (profit factor ~2, "proceed to validation"). They're real in its model — but that model uses optimistic fills and never compares to buy-and-hold. Re-run on 5-minute bars with a hold benchmark, it's a volatility-harvesting overlay riding a momentum melt-up, not an edge. It's a worse way to be long these names.

4 · What the strategy actually is

A volatility-scaled take-profit / stop system. σ = the typical daily range; each config t{X}_s{Y} sets take-profit at X·σ and stop at Y·σ. It buys dips on a Finviz momentum/semiconductor screen, takes a small profit or stops out, and holds open positions for up to ~14 days. It is the same family as the Excel volatility-adjusted dip-buy — just with a tighter stop and wider target (e.g. t1.25_s1.00 = TP 1.25σ, stop 1.0σ).

Median hold 3.1h, mean 20.6h, longest ~14 days — overnight-capable, not purely intraday. That multi-day hold is how it rides the drift.

The engine

Caveat: realistic head-to-head is a 10-name representative subset (it includes the largest movers, where the effect is strongest, plus choppy names where the strategy helps). The broader 20-name buy-and-hold averaged +48.4%. Interpreting the config as TP 1.25σ / stop 1.0σ on a standard −1σ dip entry.

6 · Bottom line

  • The summary's "validate, don't deploy" is correct — but for a stronger reason than it states.
  • The decisive test it omits — beat buy-and-hold net of realistic fills and costs — it fails: realistic +9.0% vs hold +58.7% on the same names.
  • It's a vol-harvesting overlay on momentum beta: it adds value on choppy names but throws away trend upside, and the optimistic-fill "profit" is 84% illusion.
  • Same conclusion as the Excel dip-buy — a more complex, riskier way to underperform simply holding a basket that went up.
